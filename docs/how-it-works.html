Inside Truly

A step by step tour of how Truly turns two answers into a path of music, how it chooses each piece, and how its two small on-device models hear and see. The interactive parts below run the app's own code on the real music collection.

1The big picture

Truly is a static website. There is no server code, no database and no account. Once the page has loaded, everything happens on your device.

  1. Check-inMood and energy, each 1 to 5.
  2. Camera or voiceOptional. A small, capped nudge to the start.
  3. Start pointThe answers placed on the music map.
  4. DirectionCalmer, gently brighter or stay.
  5. Planned pointsA straight line, one point per piece.
  6. PiecesOne per point, with your preferences.
  7. Play and explainA reason for every piece.

2The map: mood and energy as numbers

Truly puts people and music on one map with two axes (Russell, 1980). Valence is how pleasant or bright something feels; arousal is how lively it is. Every recording takes its valence, arousal and tempo from MCUD 1.0, a published dataset of music used in studies (Du et al., 2026), so no value is a guess.

Your answers are placed using the collection's own spread. Each answer from 1 to 5 picks a quantile of the catalogue: 8%, 25%, 50%, 75% or 92%. A mood of 3 means "the median valence of the music Truly has". Distances are measured in standard deviations of the catalogue, so a step in mood and a step in energy count the same.

    Direction and goal

    Each direction mainly moves one thing. Calmer lowers energy to the catalogue's 25% quantile and lifts mood a little. Gently brighter raises mood to at least the 70% quantile and moves energy a little towards the middle. Stay keeps the goal at the start. Truly suggests a direction from your answers, and you can always change it.

    Planned points

    A straight line runs from the start to the goal, with one point per piece. Slowing down keeps the first piece and spreads the same journey over half as many points again, so each step is smaller. Starting near your mood and moving gradually is the iso principle, a sequencing idea from music therapy practice (Heiderscheit and Madson, 2015) that changed listeners' affective state in an experiment (Starcke and von Georgi, 2024). Truly borrows the sequencing only, and makes no health claims.

    How gradual is a path?

    The four numbers under the explorer come from pathQuality(). Steps are measured on the map in standard deviations of the collection. Step variation is the spread of the step sizes divided by their average: 0 means every step is the same size. Studies of mood playlists judge paths on the same ideas, smoothness and even steps (Donnelly and Gaur, 2022). With 18 recordings the typical step is about half a standard deviation; a larger collection will make paths more even, and the tests keep paths from getting jumpier than they are today.

    4Choosing each piece

    At every planned point, each remaining piece gets a cost. Lower is better. All distances are in standard deviations of the catalogue.

    closeness (v - point.v)² + (a - point.a)² step + 0.35 × [(v - prev.v)² + (a - prev.a)²] backward + 1.0 × (part of that step going backwards)² tempo + 0.3 × ((bpm - prev.bpm) / 30)² + 0.6 if none of your preferred instruments - 0.25 if it is a favourite ± 0.24 at most from listening: played to the end, or left early

    Three rules come first: no piece twice, no performer twice, and never a piece marked "not for me". Truly then keeps the eight cheapest pieces and picks among those within 0.25 of the best, with weights 1, 1/4, 1/9 and 1/16, so the best match wins most of the time and near-ties sometimes swap. Finally the path is sorted along its direction, so it never steps backwards.

    Explaining each piece

    Each reason is written from the piece's place in the path. The first piece matches the answers. Every later piece is compared with the one before: a change of more than 0.15 standard deviations reads as calmer or livelier, brighter or more reflective. The last piece of a moving path is "where the path settles". When a preference applies, a short note follows, such as "Features piano, as you asked."

    5The player

    One <audio> element, built to keep going on poor connections.

    1. Music listFetched with a 10 second timeout and up to three attempts, then a "Try again" button.
    2. PlayIf a piece has not started after 15 seconds, Truly says the connection is slow and keeps waiting.
    3. Read aheadOnce a piece can play through, the next one starts downloading.
    4. Dropped connectionRetried once from the same position after 2 seconds, then the path moves on.
    5. ControlsArrow keys move 10 seconds, Page Up and Down 30. Lock screen and headset buttons work through the Media Session API.

    6The camera input

    Optional, off until tried, and only a small nudge to the starting mood. Faces are not a reliable guide to feelings (Barrett et al., 2019), so the result is offered as a playful guess.

    1. FrameDrawn from the video into memory. Never stored or sent.640 × 480
    2. Find the faceUltraFace RFB-320 checks 4,420 candidate boxes; the most confident above 0.7 wins.1 × 3 × 240 × 320
    3. CropA square around the face, 25% larger each side, with ImageNet colour normalisation.1 × 3 × 224 × 224
    4. Truly face modelEfficientNet-B0 scores 40 emotion categories from the EmoNet-Face taxonomy.1 × 40
    5. Read outAveraged into seven expression groups; neutral is minus the strongest group; calibration offsets added.7 classes
    6. AverageUp to ten frames 0.4 s apart. Fewer than three usable frames: no result.
    7. NudgeBright moves the start up 0.35 standard deviations of valence, heavy down 0.35, calm not at all. Energy is untouched.

    7The voice input

    Optional, off until tried. It listens to the sound of the voice and never the words: nothing is transcribed. It mainly nudges the starting energy, and mood only lightly.

    1. RecordFive seconds into memory. Echo cancellation on; noise suppression and automatic gain off.
    2. ResampleTo 16 kHz with an OfflineAudioContext.80,000 samples
    3. Loudness stepSounding parts brought to an RMS of 0.05, peaks kept under 0.99. Too quiet: no result.
    4. Truly voice modelSpectrogram and CNN, all inside one ONNX file.1 × 80000 → 1 × 2
    5. Read outShifted so 0 means "about as lively as everyday speech", then capped.
    6. NudgeEnergy moves at most 0.4 standard deviations, mood at most 0.12.
    7. ForgetThe sound buffers are overwritten with zeros.

    Why a loudness step?

    A voice close to a phone and a voice across the room differ far more in loudness than in expression. Training clips and microphone audio both go through the same step, so the model judges how lively the sound is, not how close the microphone was.

    Inside the model

    1. DFT as a convolution402 fixed filters: cosine and sine waves for 201 frequencies, times a Hann window. 400-sample window, 160-sample hop.201 × 498
    2. Mel bands64 bands from 50 Hz to 8 kHz, spaced like pitch hearing.64 × 498
    3. Log and standardisePer clip, so any remaining level difference disappears.
    4. Four conv blocksTwo 3 × 3 convolutions with batch norm and ReLU, then 2 × 2 max pooling, in each block.24 → 48 → 96 → 128
    5. Average and outputGlobal average pool over time and pitch, then one linear layer.128 → 2

    The spectrogram filters are fixed buffers, not trained weights. Writing the Fourier transform as a convolution keeps the whole model in standard ONNX operators, so the browser only passes raw samples in.

    8How the models were trained

    Both models are students distilled from larger LAION models. The small model learns to reproduce the big model's outputs on many examples, so the knowledge fits in a size that runs in a browser. Only non-clinical outputs of the teachers were ever computed.

    1. CollectImages or five-second clips from openly licensed datasets.
    2. TeacherThe large LAION model scores each one, once, on a Kaggle GPU.
    3. StudentThe small model predicts the same scores.
    4. LearnSmooth L1 loss between student and teacher; AdamW with a one-cycle schedule.
    5. TestOn people the student never saw: CREMA-D actors with odd identifiers.
    6. ExportTo ONNX, checked against PyTorch and the browser runtime.

    9What is stored, and where

    Saving is off by default. Nothing is ever sent to a server.

    In memory, for this visit

    • Mood and energy answers
    • Camera and voice results
    • Video frames and sound, cleared after use
    • Preferences, when saving is off
    only if you switch saving on

    In this browser's localStorage

    • truly.paths.v1: paths played, up to 50
    • truly.favourites.v1
    • truly.not-for-me.v1
    • truly.preferences.v1: instruments and length
    • truly.listening.v1: per piece, how often played to the end or left early

    Mood answers, images and sound are never stored anywhere. The Privacy page in the app downloads or deletes everything saved, and store.js is the only code that touches storage.
