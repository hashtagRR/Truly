# Research basis

Truly's music paths draw on published research in music, emotion and recommendation. This page summarises the ideas each part of the app uses and lists the sources. Truly is a music recommender; none of this research is used to make claims about anyone's health.

## Mood on a map

A widely used model places emotions on two dimensions: valence, from unpleasant to pleasant, and arousal, from calm to excited (Russell, 1980). Music research describes pieces on the same two dimensions, which lets a listener's answers and a piece of music sit on one map. Truly turns the mood answer into a starting valence and the energy answer into a starting arousal.

## Every piece has a place

Each recording in Truly's catalogue takes its valence, arousal and tempo from MCUD 1.0, a published dataset of music used in studies, with annotations for every song (Du et al., 2026). Using published values, rather than Truly's own guesses, keeps each piece's place on the map consistent and open to inspection.

## Start where you are

Matching music to a listener's current mood before moving towards a different one is a sequencing idea that originated in music therapy practice, where it is known as the iso principle (Heiderscheit and Madson, 2015). An experiment with listeners found that music sequenced this way changed their affective state over the course of listening (Starcke and von Georgi, 2024). Truly borrows the sequencing idea for its paths: the first piece sits near the chosen mood, and each later piece is a small step towards the chosen direction.

## A face is only a hint

A major review found that facial movements do not reliably reveal how a person feels: the same expression can accompany different feelings, and the same feeling can look different on different faces (Barrett et al., 2019). For this reason Truly's optional camera only nudges where a path starts, is described as a playful guess, and never overrides what the listener says.
